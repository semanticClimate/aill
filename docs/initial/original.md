#OKFN
 We are piloting an Accelerator for AI skills to develop thematic methodologies and curricula that could later be replicated and used by similar initiatives

The idea will be that a thematic AI Specialist - in my mind, someone from Semantic Climate- could collaborate with an organisation (Climate Academy) in helping them understand how AI is impacting their sector, but also how they can understand AI better to both take a critical approach to it and use it in their work. 

The idea would be to develop something concrete together as a result of the process - a small pilot, a short publication, a practical example and then OKFN will transform the process in a course and a curriculum translated to different languages so other organisations (with similar missions to Climate Academy) could apply it to their work. 

the objective will be not only to have dedicated thematic curricula and examples to showcase but also multilingual courses so people can understand AI applied to a specific field. 

# PMR
 This is then a balance between:

* what we can do cheaply and scalably
* what we think people would want and use.

AI is good at discovering resources - not always good at running trustable processes thereafter. And good for creating code *when overseen by a human*. Not good for designing new processes and tools without human input

Our emerging tech is a personalisable encyclopedia built in an automatic manner from open documents (i.e. no friction in access, though probably lossy). We aim at major public documents from domain-specific sites and we harvest the terms in an unsupervised manner. Then we add Wikipedia/Wikidata material, and create a knowledge graph linking related entries. We can augment this with recent scholarly publications. We are getting this down to a few hours (depending on download times). This means that if you are interested in, say, a {country, crops, climate phenomena, etc.} we can create the encyclopedia which can be customised for general use. We have already prototyped multilingual equivalences for terms and can extend to multilingual Wikipedia.

# GY

May I suggest that we treat the “concrete output” not only as a technical artefact (e.g., an automatically generated encyclopedia or knowledge graph), but as a demonstrator of a methodology. For eg  on how organisations can meaningfully engage with AI in their domain while understanding its limits, risks, and governance implications.

The strength of this collaboration could be precisely the combination of:
Technical prototyping (resource discovery, structuring knowledge, multilingual linking),
Sector interpretation (what the organisation actually needs and trusts), and
Reflective practice (how AI changes workflows, decisions, and evaluation).
In many of the communities we work with (climate, biodiversity, policy, and Global South research institutions), the main barrier is not access to AI tools but:
* knowing when to trust outputs
* documenting human oversight
* ensuring transparency and accountability
* translating AI outputs into institutional decision processes

So perhaps the pilot could take the form of a guided co-creation process with a partner organisation?
We can jointly build a small domain knowledge resource (such as the encyclopedia/graph described by PMR), but equally importantly we document:
* what worked
* what required human judgment
* where bias or uncertainty appeared
and how the organisation decided whether to use it.
That way the final deliverable naturally becomes:
a practical example + short publication + replicable curriculum (all quite aligned with OKFN’s objective!)

metaphors/cultural storytelling to communicate scientific truths

## Psychology
so much learning / engagement about 'green' things is rooted in the individual actions or extreme weather events... 
a major reason for this is a nervousness around 'being political' - an individual action is safe, it doesn't pose system questions & it is even feels virtuous inside the constellation of values in capitalism/neo-liberalism's celebration of the individual/choice. 
a focus on extreme weather is also safe - because they tend to happen elsewhere & again, it crops out the wider system questions/signals of approaching instability in Earth Systems. 
climate change/sustainability has been pushed to the margins of school, media and everywhere - it only tends to be done by 'good people' / bio apple eaters. A consequence of this is that the learning/action has to "feel good" or "be positive" for it to happen. ... because it is most done 'voluntarily' (so you need some kind of reward....) 
This marginalisation of the content also means that green things are not generally associated with technical depth/cultural depth ... which makes them quite 'boring' for the brain & for the heart... 

the focus on physical tipping points in Earth Systems in the content, is mirrored in an attentiveness to tipping points in social systems to achieve change  - or more specifically, leverage points (education, law, media... ) 
this opens up a whole new set of narratives/virtues/areas of engagement/storytelling ... confronting systems is at first disempowering, but ultimately, empowering. 

## Skills

these skills have a high value for schools/certification/individual development

# PMR

Basically sC has a generic approach which can be repurposed for many different purposes. We have a complete workflow from query -> terms -> encyclopedia -> knowledge_graph. It's embeddable in Wikidata/Wikipedia so terms become semantic and part of the larger world Linked Open Data .  We are developing a semanticCorpus based on BAGIT which I think could be called "frictionless" - ingestion of documents generates metadata which allows a variety of reuse. 
Because it's keyed into Wikidata we have a multilingual terminology and can also decorate the terms with multilingual Wikipedia descriptions. We hope that one of our collaborators in Argentina (whom we are meeting tomorrow) would be interested in a Spanish (and possibly Portuguese) extension, and the current interns can support a number of South Asian languages. (we don't translate - we use the Wikimedia addressing system).

So it's a highly flexible , automatic multilingual knowledgebase - and we can probably tune it to many projects.

We also emphasize entry-level collaborators. We have interns who have little or no computing and they "learn by doing". This process in itself is valuable as it is semi-structured - guiding entrants by assigning tasks - often in. parallel (e.g. the interns all take a different chapter of the 50 chapters in IPCC).

# MP

## A Climate Academy AI Bot (as yet, unnamed 🙂) 

## First principles 
The bot will be strictly guard railed inside reliable science. 
The first, overriding/framing principle for the bot, will be these national numbers: CUTx 
It should be tuned into a sharp awareness and prioritisation of not crossing planetary boundaries/tipping points. 
Then the details can be filled out by the latest IPCC reports & other key data/research (eg. IPBES, Emissions Gap Report. 

## Fundamental values

We are completely open/transparent about where the bot goes to get its answers. 
Everything is the best available knowledge and understanding that humanity has (IPCC etc) 
The CUTx numbers are simply the latest carbon budget / resource extraction figures, divided per capita (the most basic principle of human rights)
there are a few more common sense principles which are explained on the website (eg. the numbers are rooted in consumption, nor territory, they don't allow for 'overshoot'...) 
By limiting the bot to this rich, but narrow field, the bot will carry a tremendous level of authority and simplicity. Both powerful things. 

Individuals, Schools, Media, all do not know where to turn to for reliable answers and numbers. 
Whilst most people are aware of the blunt climate misinformation/skepticism that is fueled by fossils, there is a major fog in the public space about some absolutely critical issues: 
there is no "dashboard" for climate change - we did it with COVID - quickly, but we don't have one for this existential threat; the UN only wants to talk in generalities, national governments have all kinds of unhelpful rubrics in how they declare their commitments & individuals are fed a diet of 'individual voluntary actions'
the global north (typically the EU) understands itself as a global leader in emissions/environment , the numbers do not agree. 
there is a common consensus that we in an energy transition phase, but we are still in an energy addition phase. 
few people have a clear understanding of planetary boundaries and tipping points, and how close and how dangerous they are. (everything is mostly framed as 'increase in extreme weather events, or individual actions)
the narrative line on AI and Climate is almost always: AI carries a huge energy / water cost.
By limiting our bot to a smaller field this will surely lead to a major reduction in this. 

## Complexity ahead 

The IPCC is funded by the UN. The "Summary for Policymakers" (SYR) (especially) have the fingerprints of political interests on them. Whilst it is the most reliable science, and whilst we do hold a wonderfully deep and advanced understanding of Earth Systems, there are some glitches that need careful consideration. 
B and M are very well placed to support us with this & have agreed to do so. 
Their work has the support of the world's eminent climate scientists (cf. CUTx website & their own)


## Longer term....  

The first principle and priority is simply to make the latest and best science available to the world. But it is one thing to give people the right information, but as Peter pointed out in the call yesterday, there is another game in play and that is all the social/psychological/tribal/System 1 (Kahneman) dynamics in play.... 
The Climate Academy has been designed to try and bridge/transcend some of these gaps - through culture, narrative, community, purposeful projects.... 

The bot would be an absolutely fantastic addition to our ecosystem - on so many levels - and maybe, in the longer term, we could make the bot to be savvy about these cultural issues which are in play, without ever compromising the quality/reliability of the science... 

I am in the early stages of working with [...organization...] to sharpen and deepen their coverage of sustainability. This would be a very powerful addition to that.
